Case study · Banking data pipeline

Reconcile first, then analyse.

I combined checking-account, credit-card and customer files into one trusted dataset. Before any analysis, I audited it for quality problems and proved with control totals that every row and every dollar made it through. Then I answered the business questions: where money goes, and which customers have gone quiet.

The problem

Three systems, one question

Checking activity, card activity and customer details lived in three separate files. Answering "how much money comes in versus goes out?" means combining them, and that is where errors creep in.

  • Colliding IDs. Both transaction files number their rows from 1, so a naive merge produces duplicate keys.
  • Mixed meanings. Deposits, withdrawals and card purchases all share one "Amount" column, so adding it up gives a number with no financial meaning.
  • Silent data problems that only surface once a report is already wrong.

1 · Data-quality audit

Four problems no report would have shown

  • One in eight transactions has an impossible date: it happened before the customer opened their account. In a real bank that points to a migration error or a wrong join date, and it goes back to the data owner before anything is published.
  • More than a third of checking activity belongs to customers recorded as savings-only. Either the product field is out of date or transactions are on the wrong customer.
  • The balance column is completely empty, so no balance-based metric can be trusted from this extract.
  • Twelve card transactions fall after the reporting period and are excluded from the 2022–2024 figures.

Duplicate, orphan and zero-amount checks all passed. Every problem row stays in the model with a flag, so each excluded number can be traced back to its source.

4 · Card customers

42% of card spend came from customers who have gone quiet

  • 279 lapsed customers, with no card purchase in over a year, account for 42% of historical card spend. They are the clearest re-engagement target.
  • 63 "Champions", 11% of card users, produce 22% of spend. They are the customers to protect.
  • Purchases are infrequent. The median gap between a customer's card purchases is 256 days, so a quiet quarter is not yet a churn signal.
  • Spending is spread almost evenly across categories, and card adoption is flat across income bands (55–57%).
